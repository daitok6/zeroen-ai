## MEDIUM

### M1 — Hero Variant 1 makes an absolute "no conflict of interest" claim broader than what's supported
> **"Free Meta Ads audits have a conflict of interest. This one doesn't."**

The defensible fact (used well everywhere else in the body) is narrower: there is
**no *retainer* to protect**. A flat *"This one doesn't [have a conflict of
interest]"* is an absolute — and the messaging brief §5 explicitly warns against
certainty phrasing and says *"state the fact, let the reader draw the
conclusion."* It also isn't strictly true: a paid pilot whose acknowledged
purpose is to learn toward future products, and an auditor whose perceived value
rises with how much he "finds," both retain second-order incentives. Variant 2
("nothing to sell you," "no management contract attached") is clean and does not
have this problem.
- **Fix (route to Pam):** tighten V1's second sentence to the retainer-specific,
  structural claim — e.g. *"This one has no retainer to protect."* — matching the
  body's framing. Or default to Variant 2.

### M2 — Access & Privacy section is silent on data retention, deletion, and confidentiality
The section is clear on *write-access = never* and *purpose limitation* ("used
only to produce your audit"), but says **nothing about how long data is kept,
whether it is deleted/returned afterward, or that it won't be shared.** The buyer
is granting access to an ad account that can contain audience/spend and, via
custom audiences, personal data — across JP (APPI) and international/EU (GDPR)
buyers. This is the Phase-4 cross-cutting data-privacy risk surfacing at the point
of the ask; a privacy-conscious buyer will notice the gap, and it is light on
compliance hygiene.
- **Fix (route to Pam, founder to confirm it's actually honored):** add one plain
  line, e.g. *"I use what you share only to produce your audit, don't share it
  with anyone, and delete or return it on request."* Do **not** publish a
  retention promise the founder won't keep.

## LOW

### L1 — Copy's own "¥50,000 stated once" rule isn't literally met
The number is repeated on the landing page (Price section, FAQ, and the
paid-vs-free section D). **Not misleading** — the figure never varies — but the
brief/self-constraint said "stated plainly, once." If "once" is a real rule, drop
the price mention from the FAQ and §D and let the Price section own it. If the
intent was only "no comp-anchoring," this is fully satisfied and no change is
needed. Flagging so the founder decides which.

### L2 — Governance watch (not a copy defect): the "nothing sold afterward" promise binds future behavior
"One report, one walkthrough, nothing after" and "nothing pulling the findings
toward 'sign here'" are honest **only for as long as these 3 pilot buyers are
never later pitched O2/O4.** The approved plan captures O2/O4 interest as research
only — good — but a *future* O2 outreach that reaches a pilot customer would
retroactively make this printed promise false.
- **Route to Michael:** keep pilot buyers on an explicit do-not-pitch list for any
  later O2/O4 launch, so the promise stays true in hindsight.

### L3 — Cold-outreach personalization asserts knowledge that must be verified per-recipient
EN cold: *"I noticed you're running your own Meta Ads."* JP cold:
*"Meta広告を自社で運用されているとのことで."* These state a fact about the
recipient. Accurate only if sent **strictly** to prospects with a verified
self-managing signal; sent broadly, it becomes false personalization that erodes
the exact trust the offer sells. Targeting-discipline dependency, not a text fix.
