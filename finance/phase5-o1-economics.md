Add scoping call + access setup + follow-up ≈ **+1–2 hrs**.
→ **Total ~6–12 hrs early; ~3.5–7 hrs once templated.** Reason: sales/admin
overhead is unavoidable per customer and does NOT compress as fast as delivery.

## 4. Gross-margin implications **[ASSUMPTION]**
- **Cash cost/audit ≈ near-zero** (API free tier + existing tools) → **cash
  gross margin ~90%+.** This is misleadingly attractive.
- **True constraint = founder time, not cash.** Effective hourly rate =
  price ÷ founder hours:
  - Early: ¥50k ÷ ~9 hrs ≈ **~¥5,600/hr [ASSUMPTION/HYPOTHETICAL EXAMPLE]**
  - Templated: ¥50k ÷ ~5 hrs ≈ **~¥10,000/hr [ASSUMPTION]**
- **Finance point:** report this as *founder-hour yield*, not just cash margin.
  A 90% cash margin on a service that fully consumes founder hours is still an
  agency, not a scalable business.

## 5. Workload threshold — where it becomes unsustainable **[ASSUMPTION]**
Using ~10–15 hrs/week capacity and ~5 hrs/audit (templated):
| Audits/week | Founder hrs/wk on delivery | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~5 | Sustainable; leaves ~5–10 hrs to BUILD O2/O4. |
| 2 | ~10 | Borderline; little build time left. |
| **3+** | **~15+** | **Unsustainable — 100% of capacity on delivery, zero on automation → the trap.** |
- **Sustainable ceiling ≈ 1–2 audits/week** *if* the goal is to keep building
  the recurring product. Reason: the binding limit is not "can the founder do
  more audits" but "does audit volume starve the automation roadmap."
- Implication: O1 should be **volume-capped on purpose** (scarce slots), not
  scaled — scaling O1 is scaling an agency.

## 6. Price that makes the offer worth testing **[ASSUMPTION]**
- Must be **non-zero and non-trivial** so a purchase is genuine WTP evidence
  (a free audit proves nothing — PRINCIPLES #3).
- Must roughly justify the founder-hour opportunity cost (see §4).
- Must be low enough to close **3 paying validation buyers** without heavy
  selling.
- **Recommendation: anchor the test at ~¥50,000**, and deliberately probe
  sensitivity by also floating ~¥100,000 to a subset. Reason: the experiment's
  value is the *price-response curve*, not one price. **Cheapest validation
  (PRINCIPLES #11):** offer to a handful of prospects (Sales/founder — no
  outreach by agents) and see who pays which tier before building anything.

## 7. O1 → O2/O4 upsell economics **[ASSUMPTION]**
- The audit's real economic purpose is **acquiring a recurring customer**, so
  treat the audit price as partially **offsetting the CAC** of an O2/O4 seat.
- Illustrative recurring price (from Phase 4): **~¥15,000–30,000/mo
  [ASSUMPTION]** for monitoring/creative-performance seats.
- **LTV = monthly price × retention months** — retention is unknown; even at a
  modest few months, one converted audit buyer's recurring LTV can exceed the
  one-time audit fee. So:
  - If the audit ~covers founder time AND converts even a **fraction** of
    buyers to recurring, O1 is economically justified as a **paid acquisition
    channel** for the scalable product.
  - The scalable margin lives in **O2/O4 recurring (low marginal founder
    time)**, not in O1 itself.
- **Unknowns that decide this (must validate):** conversion rate audit→
  recurring, retention/churn, recurring price WTP. All currently zero-evidence.

## Bottom line for god/founder
- Test O1 at a **~¥50k anchor** (probe ¥30k/¥100k), **capped at ~1–2
  audits/week** to protect build time.
- Judge O1 on **founder-hour yield + conversion to recurring**, NOT cash
  margin or audit volume.
- **Scaling O1 = building an agency (the trap).** Success = O1 funds and feeds
  the O2/O4 recurring engine.
- Needed before this is more than hypothesis: Oscar [REAL] competitor audit
  pricing; Sales WTP + audit→recurring conversion signal; confirmed founder
  weekly capacity.
