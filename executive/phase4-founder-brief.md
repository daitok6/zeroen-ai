## The strongest case for — and against — each option

### O2 — Meta Ads Monitoring & Intelligence

**Strongest argument for:** It is inherently ongoing, making the recurring model and scheduled polling/change-detection workflow strong **[DESK]** fits. There is also **[REAL]** evidence of the problem environment: documented overspend, unexplained-drop, and suspension events; existing products market anomaly detection.

**Strongest argument against:** The proposed value layer is explaining *why* performance changed. The source identifies attribution noise as the largest risk: a confident but incorrect explanation can be worse than no explanation, damaging trust and potentially prompting a bad customer decision. Free Meta rules also cover the low end, and ongoing API access remains a platform risk.

**Most important missing evidence:** Whether customers will pay for explanations rather than free alerts; their false-positive tolerance; and whether the explanation can be reliably correct.

### O4 — Creative Performance Analysis

**Strongest argument for:** The source documents a paid, ongoing market and a recurring creative-testing cadence. Analysis, asset pull, tagging, and scoring have **[DESK]** moderate-to-high automation potential, while teardown content can demonstrate the product.

**Strongest argument against:** Atria already offers the bundle that had been proposed as O4’s moat—creative analysis, competitor intelligence, and $9B benchmarking. The remaining ZeroEn differentiation is therefore positioning, price, workflow, or Japan—not an open benchmarking gap. That differentiation is still unresolved.

**Most important missing evidence:** Whether advertisers will pay incrementally over Motion or Atria; the minimum spend/creative volume at which analysis is useful; Japan demand; and what concretely differentiates ZeroEn.

### O1 — Meta Ads Audit

**Strongest argument for:** It has the broadest reach, lowest trust barrier, and lowest technical difficulty **[DESK]** of the shortlist. **[REAL]** audit alternatives and small existing audit spend support an immediate paid validation path; it can generate early cash and learning.

**Strongest argument against:** It is a one-time, founder-time-intensive service in a market where agencies often provide audits free. Its strategic value relies on an untested conversion from audit buyer to O2/O4 recurring customer; the source flags a potential segment mismatch between price-sensitive audit buyers and higher-spend recurring buyers.

**Most important missing evidence:** Whether buyers will pay rather than choose a free audit; the audit-to-recurring conversion rate; and whether audit buyers match the O2/O4 target customer.

## Founder decision requested

Authorize the next validation sequence—while keeping the current shortlist intact:

1. **O2 and O4:** Run one cheap **combined shared-buyer** paid deposit/pre-commitment test; only if deposits land, proceed to manual delivery. This preserves their co-equal ranking while using real WTP to resolve the critical unknown.
2. **O1:** Offer a fixed-price paid audit as the immediate cash-and-learning wedge, collecting payment up front and tracking whether buyers convert to O2/O4-style recurring work.
3. **O5 / Japan:** Bundle reporting into the winner rather than sell it alone; authorize a JP-market research spike before treating the Japan wedge as a differentiator.

This authorization is required because prospect contact and the proposed validation experiments are consequential external actions. It is **not** authorization to build software, spend money, or commit to a final product direction.
