## 1. Ideal Early Prospect

Self-managing Meta advertiser (owner-operator or 1–3 person marketing function) running their **own**
Meta Ads, **~¥450k+/mo (~$3k+/mo), ideally $5k+/mo** — not the $1k/mo floor (launch plan §1: this is
where the audit's price-vs-recoverable-waste math actually clears). Single decision-maker, no
procurement chain. Currently **in** a trigger moment (§6), not just generically running ads.
Agencies/freelancers are **not** an outreach target for this pilot (they already give audits away
free as their own lead-magnet — a future reseller angle, untested, not now).

## 2. Qualification Criteria

Before Pam's copy invites a reply, or before the founder replies to one, the prospect should
plausibly clear: real ongoing spend at/above the band in §1 · sole or clear budget authority · at
least one live trigger from §6 · willingness in principle to share account data (read-only access or
exported reports — offer the export option up front, never make them ask). Disqualify: no real Meta
spend, full confidence with zero doubt and no trigger, or an already-resourced in-house performance
team.

## 3. Warm Outreach Objective (founder's own network)

**Goal: a direct, low-friction reply — either "I'll take a slot" or "I know someone."** Trust is
already established, so the message should read as a personal ask, not a pitch: state plainly that
this is a real, paid, capped-3 pilot (not a favor request dressed as a sale), and make it easy to say
no. Do **not** discount or soften the price for network contacts — a network "yes" at a discount
would contaminate the pilot's own falsification test (launch plan §13: non-network buyers are the
real signal). A network buyer should pay full ¥50,000 like anyone else, or the "yes" doesn't count as
evidence.

## 4. Cold Outreach Objective (community/first-contact)

**Goal: get a reply expressing interest — not an instant close.** Cold copy's job is to earn a
response worth following up on, matching the approved "apply for 1 of 3 pilot slots" framing (not a
self-serve buy button). Success = the prospect self-identifies as matching §1/§2 and asks a question
or expresses interest; the qualification/close happens in the follow-up exchange, not in the first
message. Cold copy should not try to do everything at once.

## 5. Strongest Buyer Motivations

- Suspects wasted ad spend or a mis-set-up account but has no trustworthy way to check (launch plan
  §2) — loss-aversion is the core lever, not aspiration.
- Distrust of the free-agency-audit norm once it's named — the conflict-of-interest framing (Jim's
  §5/§6) converts "why would I pay for this" into "this is the one thing free can't offer."
- Wanting confidence **before** a bigger decision: scaling spend, replacing an agency, or reporting
  results to a boss/partner — the audit reduces the risk of that decision, it isn't the goal itself.
- Wanting a second, unbiased set of eyes when they've inherited an account they didn't build.

## 6. Trigger Moments (episodic — target people mid-trigger, per launch plan §2)

- Performance recently dropped or plateaued.
- Just took over / inherited the ad account.
- Actively deciding whether to keep or replace an agency/freelancer.
- About to scale spend meaningfully and wants to de-risk first.

Outreach copy should reference these directly ("if you've recently taken over the account, or
results have stalled...") rather than a generic "struggling with ads?" opener — specificity signals
this isn't mass-blasted copy.

## 7. Likely Objections — Outreach-Length Responses

Full objection-handling copy for the landing page is Jim's §16; below is the **short, DM-length**
version Pam needs for conversational replies:

| Objection (as heard in a reply) | Short response |
|---|---|
| "Agencies already do this for free" | "Free ones are built to win you as a retainer client — mine's paid and one-time specifically so there's nothing else I'm trying to sell you." |
| "How do I know you're any good" | "Fair — I can show you a redacted example finding so you can see the format before deciding." |
| "Not sure it's worth paying for" | "Totally get it — happy to just answer questions first, no pressure either way." |
| "I'd have to give you account access" | "You can also just export/screenshot the reports yourself if that's easier — either works." |
| "Is this going to turn into a sales pitch for something else" | "No — it's one report, one walkthrough, nothing after. That's the whole pilot." |

## 8. Desired Next Step

**Cold:** a reply/DM expressing interest → short qualification exchange (§2) → invite to apply for
one of the 3 pilot slots. **Warm:** can move straight to "want one of the 3 slots, or know someone
who would?" given existing trust. Neither path should try to close a sale inside the very first
message — the first message's only job is to earn a real reply.

## 9. Follow-Up Logic

- **One follow-up only**, roughly 3–5 days after no response — do not chase further; this is
  unpaid, founder-time-constrained outreach for 3 slots, not a volume funnel.
- If the reply is "maybe later" — ask for one specific reconnect date, don't accept a vague "I'll
  think about it" as a commitment (matches the interest/intent/payment discipline in
  `sales/README.md`).
- **Post-delivery follow-up (after an audit is done) is research-only, never an offer** — this is a
  hard, approved constraint (§16–17 of the launch plan): questions like "would ongoing monitoring
  have helped?" are allowed as feedback-gathering; anything that reads as pitching O2/O4 is not,
  under any framing.
- Once 3 slots are filled, outreach stops — no waitlist-building language, no "join the waitlist for
  round 2" (that would imply a next round that isn't decided).

## 10. Recommended CTA

**"Apply for a pilot audit."** This is Jim's already-approved CTA (messaging brief §12) — reused
here, not re-derived, because outreach copy and page copy must say the same thing. Do not soften it
to "Buy now" / "Get your audit" (implies instant self-serve) or harden it to a phone-call ask (implies
more commitment than the real first step requires).

## 11. EN vs. JP Outreach Differences

- **Tone/register:** JP outreach should open with a brief, genuine politeness frame (a short
  self-introduction, acknowledgment of the unsolicited contact) before the ask — an EN cold DM can be
  more direct in the first line. Keep both honest about the paid nature of the offer per §12 — the
  register differs, the substance doesn't.
- **Directness of ask:** EN copy can state the price/CTA plainly in a short message; JP copy
  typically softens the direct ask slightly (e.g., "may I ask a few questions" before "here's the
  offer") without hiding any of the required disclosures in §12.
- **Channels differ, and confidence differs sharply between them:** EN channels have at least one
  **[REAL]**-confirmed venue (the "Facebook Ad Hacks" Facebook group) plus generally well-known
  subreddits/LinkedIn (launch plan §10). **JP venues are [ASSUMPTION] — unconfirmed this pass.** Do
  not have Pam draft channel-specific JP copy (e.g., for a named community) until Oscar confirms a
  real venue; a generic JP DM/email template is fine to prepare now.
- **Price framing stays identical:** ¥50,000 stated plainly in both languages — no currency-based
  differential pricing, no JP-specific discount.

## 12. What Should NOT Be Said During Outreach

Full claims-to-avoid list for customer-facing copy generally is Jim's §15 — **consumed, not
repeated in full.** Outreach-specific emphasis (short messages make these easier to slip into
accidentally):

- **Never mention, hint at, or tease O2 (monitoring) or O4 (creative analysis)** — not in the first
  message, not in follow-up, not post-delivery. This is the single hardest constraint in the whole
  brief because it's the easiest one to break casually in a conversational reply ("this could turn
  into something ongoing...") — don't.
- No performance guarantees or implied outcomes ("this will fix your ROAS").
- No customer counts, testimonials, or "clients love this" — none exist yet; a redacted **sample
  finding** is the only permitted proof element (Jim §14).
- No committed turnaround day-count (Jim §8/launch plan L1) — say "fast" if needed, not a number.
- No refund/satisfaction guarantee unless the founder has explicitly pre-approved one (§18 of the
  launch plan — not yet granted as of this brief).
- No Fiverr/agency price anchoring or discount framing ("cheaper than an agency at $1,500+") — Jim's
  §10 explicitly rules this out even in short outreach copy; let independence/depth carry the price.
- No "limited time" hype language — the 3-slot cap is a real, statable constraint; treat it as fact,
  not urgency marketing.
- No superlatives beyond the one structural independence claim in Jim's §5 ("no retainer to
  protect") — no "best," "only," "most trusted."
