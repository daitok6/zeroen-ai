### Problem

You're running your own Meta Ads spend, and something's nagging at you — performance dropped or
plateaued, you just inherited the account, you're weighing whether to keep or replace your agency,
or you're about to scale spend and want a second set of eyes first. You don't have a trustworthy,
independent way to check whether the account is actually set up well or whether spend is being
wasted.

The obvious next step — asking an agency for a free audit — runs into the same problem it's supposed
to solve. A free audit is a sales tool built to win you as a retainer client. That doesn't make every
agency dishonest, but it does mean the incentive is to find *just enough* to make signing worthwhile
— not necessarily to give you the full, unflattering picture, especially if the fix would implicate
the agency's own prior work.

### Who This Is For

- You're running your **own** Meta Ads — owner-operator, or a 1–3 person marketing function — with
  real, ongoing spend, roughly **¥450,000+/month** and up.
- You're the person who can act on what the audit finds — sole or shared decision-maker, not
  waiting on a procurement chain.
- You're in one of these moments right now: results dropped or plateaued, you just took over the
  account, you're deciding whether to keep or replace an agency, or you're about to scale spend and
  want to de-risk first.

### Who This Is Not For

- You're not currently spending on Meta Ads.
- You already have a well-resourced in-house performance team doing this kind of review internally.
- You're part of a larger organization where this decision needs to go through several layers of
  approval — the pilot is built for a single, fast decision-maker.

### What the Audit Includes

A single, prioritized findings report, covering:

- **Measurement health** — Pixel and Conversions API (CAPI) setup
- **Account structure** and campaign organization
- **Audience** overlap and fragmentation
- **Creative fatigue**
- **Placements**
- **Spend efficiency** — where budget looks wasted or misallocated
- **Bid strategy**

Every finding is ranked by estimated impact and paired with a specific, concrete action you can take
yourself. Plus a **live walkthrough** where I go through the findings with you and answer questions.

### What the Audit Does Not Include

- I don't implement the fixes or make changes to your account — that's different work, and it's not
  what this is.
- I never get write access to your ad account. Read-only, or your own exports — nothing more.
- This isn't ongoing monitoring or account management. It's a one-time, point-in-time review.
- No competitor or market-wide creative benchmarking.
- No performance guarantees. I won't tell you what your ROAS will do after you act on the findings —
  nobody honest can promise that from a one-time review.

### Why Independent

This is a paid, one-time review with no retainer attached — which means there's no ongoing
relationship for me to protect by softening what I find, and no future contract riding on you liking
the report. That's a structural fact about how the engagement is built, not a claim about my
character. It's also the one thing a free agency audit can't structurally offer, because a free
audit's business model depends on you signing something afterward.

Worth saying plainly: this is new. I haven't run enough of these yet to hand you a track record. What
I can offer instead is the structure of the engagement itself, and a sample finding below so you can
judge the format before you decide.

### Why Paid, Not Free

Free audits aren't actually free — someone pays for the analyst's time. When you don't, the agency's
hoped-for future retainer does, and that's exactly the arrangement that creates the incentive to find
a reason to sign you rather than tell you the unflattering truth. Paying for this audit removes that
incentive on my side. You're not paying more for the same thing — you're paying so the incentive
runs in your direction instead of the analyst's.

This is a **pilot capped at 3 customers.** That's a real limit on how many audits I can do well by
hand in a given stretch of time — not a countdown clock or manufactured urgency. When the 3 slots are
filled, applications close.

### FAQ

**How do I know your findings will actually be right?**
Ask to see a sample finding first (below) — that shows you the format and depth before you decide,
rather than asking you to trust an abstract promise.

**Is this going to turn into a pitch for something else afterward?**
No. One report, one walkthrough, nothing after. That's the entire engagement. If I ever follow up
post-audit, it's to ask what was useful — not to sell you anything.

**Why should I pay ¥50,000 when agencies offer this for free?**
See the section above — the short version is that free audits carry an incentive to sell you a

**What if I don't want to give you access to my ad account?**
You don't have to. Exported reports or screenshots work fine — whichever is easier for you.

**Will you ever get write access to my account?**
No. Read-only access, or your own exports. I never change anything in your account.

**Is there a guarantee?**
No performance guarantee — nobody honest can promise what happens to your results after you act on

**How long does it take?**
I'm not committing to a specific number of days for this first pilot round — I'd rather tell you
honestly once I've delivered a few of these than promise a date up front and miss it.

**Why only 3 slots?**
Because I do this by hand, and I'd rather do 3 well than 10 badly. It's a real capacity limit, not a
sales tactic.

### Access & Privacy

To do the audit, I need one of two things: **read-only** access to your Meta Ads Manager account, or

## D) Paid-vs-Free — Customer-Facing Answer

**"Why should I pay ¥50,000 when agencies audit my account for free?"**

Because free isn't actually free — it's paid for differently. When an agency runs a free audit,
someone still pays for that analyst's time; if it isn't you, it's the retainer they're hoping to
win afterward. That's not a knock on every agency — plenty of the people doing free audits are
competent and mean well — but the incentive built into a free audit is still to find *enough* to
justify signing you, not necessarily to hand you the complete, unflattering picture, especially if
part of the problem is the agency's own earlier work.

Paying for this audit changes what I have to gain from it. There's no retainer waiting on the other
side of the report, so there's nothing pulling the findings toward "sign here" instead of toward
what's actually true. You're not paying for something better than what a free audit *could* be in
theory — you're paying so the incentive points at your interest instead of the analyst's.

## E) Sample Finding

---

**Finding: Conversions API not implemented — pixel-only measurement is likely undercounting
conversions**

**What we found:** The ad account is relying on browser-side Pixel tracking only. No server-side
Conversions API (CAPI) connection is present. Recent iOS/browser tracking restrictions mean
pixel-only setups typically miss a meaningful share of real conversions — the ad platform is
optimizing delivery against incomplete data.

**Estimated impact:** High. When measurement itself is incomplete, every downstream decision — which
campaigns look like they're working, where budget gets shifted, which creative gets scaled — is
being made on a partial picture. This is usually one of the highest-leverage fixes available because
it affects every other number in the account, not just one campaign.

**Recommended action:** Set up Conversions API alongside the existing Pixel, matched on the same
event names and dedup keys, so server-side and browser-side signals reconcile rather than
double-count. This is a one-time technical setup, typically achievable without a developer if using
a supported e-commerce platform's native integration.

**Why this is prioritized first:** Fixing account structure or creative before fixing measurement
means optimizing against a partially blind signal — the order matters, not just the list of issues.
