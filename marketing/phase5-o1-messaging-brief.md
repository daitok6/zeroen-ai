## 1. Target Customer
Self-managing Meta advertiser — owner-operator or 1–3 person marketing function — running their
**own** Meta Ads with real, ongoing spend. **Target the higher spend band: ~¥450k+/mo (~$3k+/mo),
ideally $5k+/mo**, not the $1k/mo floor — the audit's value math only clears there, and higher
spend means a sharper trigger and real budget authority. Single decision-maker, no procurement
chain.

## 2. Trigger Moment
Episodic, not steady-state — copy should target people mid-trigger, not "always-on" pain:
- Performance dropped or plateaued
- Just took over / inherited the account
- Deciding whether to keep or replace an agency
- About to scale spend and wants to de-risk first

## 3. Customer Problem
They suspect budget is being wasted or the account is mis-set-up, but have **no trustworthy,
independent way to check.** The obvious alternative — a free agency audit — is a biased sales
funnel built to win a retainer, not to give the full honest picture (especially where the fix
would implicate the agency's own prior work).

## 4. Value Proposition
**One-liner:** *"A second opinion on your Meta Ads account from someone with nothing to sell you."*
Longer form if a second line is needed: one-time, human-reviewed audit — no retainer, no upsell
call, just what to fix first.

## 5. Primary Differentiator
**Independence, framed as structural fact, not a claim.** A one-time paid review with no retainer
attached has no financial incentive to either flatter the account or manufacture urgency. This is
the *one thing* a free agency audit is structurally incapable of offering. Note for Pam: this is
also the plan's single **unproven hypothesis** (§13/C2 of the launch plan) — copy should assert it
plainly (it's true by construction) but the brief does not authorize inflating it with unearned
certainty language ("the only truly unbiased audit," "guaranteed unbiased") — state the fact, let
it stand on its own.

## 6. Why Paid, Not Free
Because free is the incumbent norm *and* the source of the bias problem: free audits work as lead
magnets precisely because someone eventually has to pay for the analyst's time — and when the
customer doesn't pay, the agency's future retainer does, which is what creates the incentive to
sell rather than tell the truth. Paying removes that incentive. This is the honest answer to "why
should I pay for something I can get free" — reframe, don't dodge.

## 7. Offer Structure
A **capped pilot of 3 slots** — "apply for 1 of 3 pilot slots," not a self-serve checkout. This is
a real constraint (founder-hour ceiling), not manufactured scarcity, and copy can say so honestly.
No discovery-call pitch beyond qualification; no O2/O4 offer inside the pilot flow.

## 8. Deliverables
A single **prioritized findings report** (human-reviewed) covering: measurement health (Pixel +
CAPI), account structure, audience overlap, creative fatigue, placements, spend-efficiency /
wasted-spend read, bid strategy — each finding tied to an estimated impact and a concrete,
advertiser-executable fix. Plus a **live walkthrough** of the findings.
**Turnaround:** do not state a day-count in copy yet (plan target is 2–3 business days, but zero
audits have been delivered — under-promise until founder confirms after the first one or two).

## 9. Exclusions (state plainly, not defensively)
- No fixes performed / no implementation work (that would be agency-style delivery)
- No write access to the ad account, ever
- No ongoing management or monitoring (that's a separate, not-yet-built product — never named/sold
  here)
- No competitor/market creative benchmarking
- No performance guarantees
- No refund/satisfaction guarantee (unless founder explicitly approves one before it's offered —
  do not include one by default)

## 10. Price
**¥50,000, stated plainly, once.** Do not anchor against Fiverr ($70–100) or discount-frame against
agency pricing ($1,500–5,000) in customer-facing copy — that invites a race-to-the-bottom
comparison the offer isn't built to win. Let independence + depth be the reason, not the price
delta.

## 11. Pilot Cap
**3 customers, explicitly stated as a real cap** (not "limited time" hype language). When slots
fill, the page should say so and stop collecting applicants — Pam's copy should include a natural
"pilot full" state, not just a live CTA with no off-switch.

## 12. Primary CTA
**"Apply for a pilot audit"** — deliberately not "Buy now" / "Get your audit." Signals
qualification + scarcity accurately (matches the real capped-3, discovery-first process) rather
than implying instant self-serve purchase.

## 13. Tone
Direct, plain-spoken, quietly confident — not hypey, not apologetic about being new or unproven.
Lead with the honest tension (free audits have a conflict of interest) rather than a generic pain
headline. Treat "we're new" as a stated fact, not something to hide behind confidence-cosplay
(no invented authority, no borrowed-trust language).

## 14. Claims We May Safely Make
- "One-time, paid, independent audit — no retainer, no ongoing contract."
- "We don't do the fixes for you or manage your account — you keep the findings and the action
  plan."
- Exact scope of what's reviewed (§8 list) — this is factual and controllable.
- "This is a pilot, capped at 3 customers" — true and a legitimate scarcity signal.
- Founder's real EN/JP + hands-on Meta Ads background (concrete specifics, not vague credibility
  claims).
- A **redacted/composite example finding** clearly labeled as illustrative (shows report *format*,
  not a real client result) — permitted because it's labeled honestly.

## 15. Claims We Must Avoid
- Any performance guarantee or implied outcome ("we'll fix your ROAS," "you'll save X%").
- Any customer count, testimonial, case study, or "clients love us" language — none exist yet.
- Any specific savings/ROI number not tied to a real, disclosed audit.
- Any refund/satisfaction guarantee not pre-approved by the founder.
- Any mention, hint, or pitch of O2 (monitoring) or O4 (creative analysis) as available products
  or a next step — per the launch plan's C1 resolution, the "nothing to sell you afterward" promise
  is only honest if nothing is sold or teased afterward. If post-audit copy (e.g. a follow-up email)
  asks about ongoing needs, it must be framed as **feedback/research**, never an offer or pitch.
- Superlatives about the offer's uniqueness beyond the one structural fact in §5 (no "the best," "the
  only," "the most trusted").
- A committed turnaround day-count (see §8).

## 16. Key Objections the Copy Should Pre-Empt
| Objection | How copy should handle it |
|---|---|
| "Agencies give this away free" | Name the conflict of interest directly (§6) — don't wait for the prospect to raise it. |
| "How do I know your findings are right?" | Show the labeled redacted/composite sample finding (§14), not an abstract promise. |
| "It's one-time — not worth a real budget line" | Frame against the cost of an undiscovered fixable issue vs. the flat price, without inventing a specific savings number. |
| "I don't want to share account access" | Offer the export/screenshot alternative up front, in the same breath as mentioning access — don't make them ask. |
| "Why you / who are you?" | Concrete specifics only (§14) — no fabricated authority. Being new is stated, not concealed. |
| "What happens after the audit — are you going to pitch me something?" | Copy should proactively state the pilot has no follow-on sales pitch attached — this pre-empts the skepticism the whole positioning depends on defeating. |
