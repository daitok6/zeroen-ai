**Evidence discipline:** [REAL] = cited public source · [DESK] = reasoned from public patterns ·

## O1 — Meta Ads performance auditing (one-time/recurring audit)

- **Buyer segment:** [ASSUMPTION] Two distinct buyers, not one — (a) self-managing advertisers
  ($1K–$50K/mo spend) unsure if their account is healthy, and (b) agencies who would want an
  audit tool to sell audits to *their own* clients (a wholesale/white-label angle, unconfirmed).
- **Positioning angle:** "An independent second opinion on your account" — the core challenge is
  that a free/low-cost audit is already the most over-used lead-magnet mechanic in this industry
  (agencies use it to sell services); differentiation has to be *no-strings, no-sales-call*
  framing, not the audit format itself.
- **Channels — EN:** SEO content ("meta ads audit checklist"); organic value-first posts in
  r/PPC and r/FacebookAds — both real, active communities [REAL: stackmatix.com, raultiru.com
  "Best Marketing Subreddits 2026" confirm both exist and are used by advertisers/agencies for
  troubleshooting and research; exact member counts not verified]; a landing page offering a free
  audit, promoted via founder's LinkedIn; cold outbound to accounts with visibly poor public
  signals (Meta Ad Library is public and free to browse).
- **Channels — JP:** Sponsored/guest content on MarkeZine, a real, established JP digital-marketing
  publication that has run Facebook-ads-specific articles and event listings [REAL:
  markezine.jp, kokuchpro.com list Facebook-ads seminars/勉強会]; note.com articles; JP marketing
  accounts on X.
- **Acquisition difficulty: LOW–MEDIUM.** Read-only ask (no account write access needed) keeps
  the trust barrier low, and "free audit" is a well-understood funnel shape prospects already
  recognize — easy to get initial interest, harder to stand out from the same-shaped offer
  everyone already runs.
- **Best wedge channel:** Landing page + organic Reddit/LinkedIn content + light outbound,
  functioning as a lead-gen funnel into O2/O4 rather than a standalone destination (matches
  Oscar's "on-ramp" read).

## O2 — Meta Ads monitoring & intelligence

- **Buyer segment:** [DESK] Agencies managing 5+ accounts, or individual advertisers spending
  >$50K/mo — the point at which Oscar's research found third-party tools become "table stakes"
  over free native rules.
- **Positioning angle:** "Know before your client does" — anomaly detection *plus* an explanation
  of why it happened and what to do next, not just an alert (the layer Meta's free rules don't
  provide).
- **Channels — EN:** Meta's official Business Partner directory, a real listing mechanism for
  vetted third-party ad tools that gives inbound credibility with agencies [REAL: replyrush.com,
  branch.io confirm the directory and its role as a trust signal]; agency-focused
  communities/Slack groups and the Paid Search Association's newsletter ecosystem [REAL:
  paidsearchassociation.substack.com exists and curates PPC industry links]; LinkedIn content
  aimed at agency owners; case-study-led outbound.
- **Channels — JP:** JP 代理店 (agency) associations and MarkeZine; Japan B2B trust in this
  segment leans heavily on referral rather than cold channels [ASSUMPTION — general JP B2B
  pattern, not verified for this specific niche].
- **Acquisition difficulty: MEDIUM–HIGH.** Narrower buyer (agencies/high-spenders only), a
  recurring commitment, and read access to a live, revenue-critical ad account all raise the
  trust bar; needs proof (case studies, a working demo) before most agencies will connect an
  account, which lengthens the sales cycle relative to a one-time audit.
- **Best wedge channel:** Meta Partner directory listing + agency-community case-study content —
  credibility-led, not volume-led.

## O3 — Advertising creative generation

- **Buyer segment:** [DESK] Small–mid advertisers wanting a steady stream of new ad creative
  variations without an in-house creative team.
- **Positioning angle:** Hard to win on features alone — Oscar's research found the category is
  actively trust-damaged (billing/cancellation complaints against incumbents) and Meta gives
  generative creative away free. Any positioning has to lead with transparent billing and
  verifiable output quality, not just "AI-generated ads."
- **Channels — EN:** Product Hunt launch, faceless before/after creative-result content
  (TikTok/YouTube Shorts), creator partnerships in the AI-ad-tools niche.
- **Channels — JP:** JP creative agencies as referral partners, note.com, X JP ad-creator
  community.
- **Acquisition difficulty: HIGH.** Crowded category, free Meta-native substitute, and a
  reputationally noisy incumbent set that raises prospect skepticism before ZeroEn even makes a
  claim; standalone paid acquisition in "AI ad tools" is also a competitively expensive keyword
  space [ASSUMPTION on CPC levels — not directly checked].
- **Best wedge channel:** None strong standalone; only viable as a bundled add-on to O4, riding
  on that trust relationship rather than being sold cold.

## O4 — Creative performance analysis

- **Buyer segment:** [DESK] Agencies and brands with meaningful spend who want to know which
  creative themes are working — benchmarked against the market, not just their own account
  (Oscar's front-runner candidate; least free-substitutable).
- **Positioning angle:** "See what's actually working — not just in your account, across the
  market" — direct answer to Oscar's finding that the closest incumbent (Motion) stays
  account-siloed with no competitive benchmark.
- **Channels — EN:** Content-led growth is a strong natural fit here — public Meta Ad Library
  data can be turned into "ad teardown" content (a well-established, shareable content format)
  distributed via LinkedIn, YouTube/Shorts, and SEO; creative/UGC agency partnerships.
- **Channels — JP:** JP-language creative teardown content is a genuine gap — competitive
  creative-analysis content is less established as a practice in the JP market than in EN
  [ASSUMPTION, based on absence of JP-language equivalents of Motion/Atria found in Oscar's pass];
  distribution via note.com and JP creative-studio partnerships.
- **Acquisition difficulty: MEDIUM.** Requires building "we understand what wins" credibility
  before conversion, but the content format needed to build that credibility (teardowns) is
  inherently SEO/share-friendly and doubles as the product demo, which should compound CAC down
  over time rather than requiring paid spend per lead.
- **Best wedge channel:** Recurring public ad-teardown content series (EN and JP) — cheapest
  channel that also proves the product's core capability.

## O5 — Reporting (client/stakeholder reporting automation)

- **Buyer segment:** [REAL, via Oscar] Agencies specifically — Oscar's research found the most
  quantified pain of any candidate ("1–3 hours per client" spent on manual reporting).
- **Positioning angle:** Standalone, this collides directly with a hyper-commoditized incumbent
  set (Swydo, AgencyAnalytics, etc., per Oscar); differentiation has to come from either JP
  localization or bundling into O2/O4 rather than "better reporting" alone.
- **Channels — EN:** Extremely high, already-existing search demand ("facebook ads reporting
  template," "agency reporting automation"), making SEO cheap to justify but expensive to win
  against entrenched incumbents; direct comparison landing pages ("X alternative"); agency
  community discussion.
- **Channels — JP:** The clearer wedge — Oscar flagged that US reporting incumbents are EN-first;
  a JP-language stakeholder-report generator for JP agencies serving JP clients is comparatively
  uncontested [ASSUMPTION — competitor absence inferred, not directly confirmed for JP].
- **Acquisition difficulty: LOW for attention, HIGH for differentiated conversion.** The pain is
  well-known and easy to reach people around, but converting that attention into paying,
  differentiated customers is hard in a commoditized, price-competitive category — this is a
  reach-vs-monetization split, not a single difficulty score.
- **Best wedge channel:** JP-market SEO/localization, or bundling into O2/O4's funnel rather than
  running reporting as a standalone acquisition target.

## O6 — Optimization workflows (writes to the ad account)

- **Buyer segment:** [DESK] Agencies/advertisers wanting automated bid/budget changes beyond

- **Positioning angle:** Any positioning has to address financial liability and trust head-on
  before features — this is the one candidate where the *ask* (write access to spend money) is
  itself the main barrier, independent of how good the positioning is.
- **Channels:** Same theoretical channels as O2 (agency communities, Partner directory), but in
  practice this kind of access is very rarely granted through a cold or self-serve channel —

- **Acquisition difficulty: HIGH–VERY HIGH.** Trust barrier plus real financial/liability
  exposure makes this poorly suited to scalable marketing channels; it is closer to a high-touch,
  referral-only sale than a marketing-acquisition play.
- **Best wedge channel:** None at scale — referral from an existing O2/O4 relationship only.

## Cross-candidate acquisition pattern

The clearest split in this analysis is not demand vs. no-demand (Oscar already covered that) —
it's **trust-barrier vs. reach**. Read-only, one-time asks (O1, O4-as-content) are cheap to get in
front of people and low-risk to ask for. Recurring, account-connected asks (O2) cost more to
acquire because they need proof first. Write-access asks (O6) are barely marketing-acquirable at
all — they're relationship sales. Commoditized-but-painful categories (O5) are cheap to get
attention for and hard to convert profitably, which is a genuinely different problem from "hard to
reach" — flagging this split so it isn't collapsed into a single difficulty number in synthesis.

## Jim's ranking — 3–4 easiest-to-acquire opportunities

1. **O1 — Performance audit.** Broadest addressable buyer, lowest trust barrier (read-only,
   one-time), and the channels (SEO, Reddit, LinkedIn, outbound) are well-understood and
   low-cost to start. Best entry point into the funnel regardless of which product wins long-term.
2. **O4 — Creative performance analysis.** Content-led growth (ad teardowns) is both the
   cheapest acquisition channel available to any candidate here *and* doubles as the product
   demo — acquisition cost should compound down over time rather than staying flat.
3. **O5 — Reporting, JP-localized or bundled.** Top-of-funnel attention is the easiest of any
   candidate (search demand is real and already quantified) — flagged as easy-to-*reach* even
   though standalone monetization is hard; ranks here on acquisition difficulty specifically, not
   on business quality.
4. **O8 — JP-localized wedge (layered onto O2/O4/O5).** Not a standalone product, but the
   least-contested channel space of anything analyzed — lower competition should mean a lower
   cost per qualified lead than the equivalent EN-market play, leveraging a real founder
   advantage (EN/JP bilingual, per COMPANY.md).

**Hardest to acquire, in order:** O7 (broad, incumbent-saturated, unsharpenable positioning) >
O6 (write-access trust barrier, referral-only) > O3 (trust-damaged category, free-substitutable) >
O2 (real market but narrow buyer + long trust-building cycle).

This ranking is acquisition-difficulty only — it does not weigh in on unit economics (Kevin),
technical difficulty (Oscar), or actual willingness-to-pay (Dwight); those must be combined with
this file in Michael's synthesis, not substituted for it.
